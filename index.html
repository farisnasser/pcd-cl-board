Step 1 · The starting point

Gradient descent walks downhill

Training lowers a loss L(θ) by stepping against its gradient:

θ ← θ − η ∇L(θ)

A first-order Taylor expansion says why this works: moving by Δ changes the loss by about ∇L(θ)ᵀΔ. With Δ = −η∇L, the change is −η‖∇L‖², which is negative. The sign of a dot product decides whether a loss goes up or down. Everything that follows uses only this fact.

Step 2 · The problem

Learning task B undoes task A

Now train on task A, then on task B. While training on B, task A's loss changes by about

ΔLA ≈ −η gAᵀ gB

When the two gradients point more than 90° apart (gAᵀgB < 0), every step that helps B hurts A. This is catastrophic forgetting: a geometric event that repeats step after step.

Step 3 · The idea · try it

Put a wall in direction space

GEM (2017) looks for the direction closest to the new task's gradient that does not move the old task's loss the wrong way. PCD (Varam & AlHajri, 2026) goes further and asks the old task for a fraction τ of real progress:

mind̃ ½‖d̃ − g̃₀‖² s.t. g̃₁ᵀd̃ ≥ τ‖g̃₁‖²

Here g̃ are gradients normalized by a running average of their own size, so every one has length about 1 and τ means the same thing for any loss. The constraint is a wall, and d̃ is g̃₀ slid onto it. τ = 0 is exactly GEM.

Drag the two sliders. Past the threshold τ > (1 − c²)/(−c), the step that protects the old task makes the new task's loss go up to first order. τ is the stability–plasticity dial.

Step 4 · One training step of PCD-CL

Five operations per mini-batch

Each old task j contributes one constraint. In the rehearsal form its gradient comes from 32 stored examples of that task. In the regularization form it is the gradient of an EWC penalty, which needs no stored data. The QP is solved in its dual over the k×k Gram matrix (k = number of old tasks), so the cost grows with the number of tasks, not with the millions of parameters.

1 · Gradientsg₀ for the new task (+ replay); one gj per old task
→
2 · Normalizeg̃ = g / √EMA(‖g‖²): every objective has length about 1
→
3 · Solve the QPclosest d̃ to g̃₀ that clears every wall g̃jᵀd̃ ≥ τ‖g̃j‖²
→
4 · Rescaled = d̃ · ‖g₀‖ / ‖d̃‖ keeps the new task's step size
→
5 · SGD stepθ ← θ − η d, with plain SGD (Adam would break the sign guarantee)
Step 5 · What we test

Four questions nobody has answered for continual learning

RO1 · Is it really PCD?Shared

Our solver against the professor's code, closed forms and KKT conditions, and its reductions to GEM, A-GEM and ε-SOFT-GEM. The status is live in the Results tab.

RO2 · Does a margin help?Faris · rehearsal form

Does τ > 0 with per-task normalized walls forget less than GEM and ER, without hurting learning?

RO3 · Does τ beat λ?Salah · regularization form

With old tasks as EWC penalties and the same small tuning budget, does PCD's τ beat EWC's λ when the penalty's scale is unknown?

RO4 · What happens at task switches?Salah leads

The new gradient's size jumps at each switch. Should the EMA statistics reset, carry over, or be kept per task?

A clean "it doesn't help" is a valid result. The toy rung's real numbers are on each Results tab, in the margin chart. Your own objectives are outlined.
