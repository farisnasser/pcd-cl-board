Old tasks as walls.
Does a margin help? PCD-CL: Priority-Constrained Descent for Continual Learning

We make the new task the primary objective and every past task a constraint, then test under one frozen, pre-registered protocol what the margin, the primary and a priority hierarchy each buy. The outcome is mostly negative, and fully reported.

American University of Sharjah

TL;DR

A network that learns tasks one after another tends to lose the earlier ones. Priority-Constrained Descent (PCD) follows a primary gradient as closely as possible while requiring a scale-normalized fraction τ of first-order progress on every secondary objective. We apply it to continual learning: the new task is the primary, and every past task is a secondary, a wall the update may not cross. With τ = 0 this is algorithmically Gradient Episodic Memory (GEM), and a normalized positive margin already exists in ε-SOFT-GEM, so nothing here is a new constraint. What we add is a controlled comparison: hypotheses written before the runs, every setting fixed on a validation split, and one rule for reading every difference. Under plain SGD the result is mostly negative. Under Adam, examined more briefly, the Split CIFAR-100 findings return, and the margin still helps on Split MNIST.

396 final test-split runs 44 arms × 3 seeds × 3 class orders +1.8 ± 1.7 points of final accuracy, PCD with a margin minus Experience Replay Split MNIST · supported, but not once ER is retuned +0.4 ± 1.5 points, PCD minus Experience Replay Split CIFAR-100, 500 stored · not separated +1.7 ± 0.9 points, PCD-EWC minus EWC, the one consistent gain task-incremental · supported

The problem

Forgetting is the sign of a dot product.

A neural network trained on one task and then on a second usually loses much of what it knew about the first: catastrophic forgetting. Continual learning asks one model to learn each new task (plasticity) and keep the old ones (stability). While it trains on a task, it no longer sees the data of earlier tasks, except for what a method keeps.

One first-order fact carries the whole study. A small step θ ← θ − ηd along a direction d changes a smooth loss L by

\[ L(\theta-\eta d)-L(\theta)\;\approx\;-\eta\,\nabla L(\theta)^{\top}d . \]

So the loss goes down when ∇L⊤d > 0 and up when it is negative, for small enough steps. Plain training on the new task steps along its own gradient g0. If an old task's gradient g1 conflicts with it, g1⊤g0 < 0, every small step on the new task raises the old task's loss.

Most methods settle the trade-off between new and old with one coefficient: how much stored data is replayed, or the weight of a penalty. A coefficient mixes the two goals without saying which comes first, and its meaning depends on the raw size of each loss.

Three numbers summarize a run. Final accuracy AT: how good the final model is on every task. Forgetting FT: how far each old task has fallen from its best earlier value. Learning accuracy LA: how well each task was learned when it was new. A method can forget less simply by learning each new task less well, so forgetting is always read together with learning accuracy.

What existing methods do

The baselines of this study, and where its constraint comes from.

  1. Experience Replay (ER)Trains, at every step, on the current minibatch together with a minibatch drawn from a small memory of stored examples.
  2. Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC)Adds a penalty for moving parameters that mattered for old tasks, with one hand-tuned weight λ.
  3. Gradient Episodic Memory (GEM)Takes the direction closest to the new task's gradient that satisfies gj⊤d ≥ 0 for every old task: one wall through the origin per task. Its released code adds a memory strength γ ≥ 0 that pushes the step further into the allowed region.
  4. A-GEM and ε-SOFT-GEMA-GEM uses one wall on an aggregate memory gradient. ε-SOFT-GEM gives that wall a positive margin on unit-normalized gradients.

The method

The new task leads. Each old task draws a line.

Priority-Constrained Descent (Varam and AlHajri, 2026) is an optimizer for problems in which one objective is the main one and the others are conditions. It has one primary gradient g0 and k secondary gradients g1, …, gk. It first brings every gradient to a comparable length, written g̃i, and then solves a small quadratic program:

\[ \begin{aligned} \tilde d^{\,*}\;=\;\;&\arg\min_{\tilde d\in\mathbb{R}^p}\ \tfrac12\lVert \tilde d-\tilde g_0\rVert^2 \\ &\text{s.t.}\quad \tilde g_j^{\top}\tilde d\;\ge\;\tau_j\,\lVert \tilde g_j\rVert^2,\qquad j=1,\dots,k . \end{aligned} \]

The objective keeps the direction as close as possible to the primary's. Each constraint says: give secondary j at least a fraction τj ∈ [0, 1] of the first-order progress it would make by following its own gradient. With τj = 0 it reads "do not hurt j", which on raw gradients is exactly GEM's wall. A positive margin moves the wall away from the origin and asks the secondary to improve.

With one secondary the solution has a closed form:

\[ \tilde d=\tilde g_0+\lambda\,\tilde g_1, \qquad \lambda=\max\!\big(0,\;\tau-\tilde g_1^{\top}\tilde g_0/\lVert \tilde g_1\rVert^2\big). \]

If the primary already gives the secondary its share, λ = 0 and PCD does not bend the step at all. Otherwise it adds just enough of g̃1 to land on the wall. The primary pays for this in its own first-order progress, so the margin is an explicit stability–plasticity dial.

Why τ means the same for every loss. If one loss is 100 times larger than another, so is its gradient. PCD therefore divides every gradient by a running estimate of its typical length:

\[ \begin{gathered} v_{i,s}=\beta\,v_{i,s-1}+(1-\beta)\,\lVert g_{i,s}\rVert^2, \\ \hat v_{i,s}=\frac{v_{i,s}}{1-\beta^{s}},\qquad \tilde g_{i,s}=\frac{g_{i,s}}{\sqrt{\hat v_{i,s}+\varepsilon}} . \end{gathered} \]

Multiply a loss by any c > 0 and its normalized gradient is unchanged, as long as c²v̂ ≫ ε. That condition matters later: it fails for tiny gradients, where ε dominates.

The step keeps the primary's raw length, d = d̃ ‖g0‖ / ‖d̃‖, and is taken with plain SGD. Rescaling keeps the sign of every dot product but not the exact margin.

One training step

While the model trains on task t, with t − 1 past tasks.

  1. PrimaryCompute the new task's gradient g0. In the rehearsal form the new minibatch is, by default, joined by a replay minibatch from the memory.
  2. WallsCompute one gradient gj per past task: on its stored examples, or on its EWC penalty.
  3. NormalizeDivide each gradient by the running estimate of its own length.
  4. ProjectSet the margins τj and solve the program for d̃*. Only active walls change the step.
  5. StepRescale to ‖g0‖ and take a plain SGD step.

PCD for continual learning

One wall per past task, in two forms.

While the model trains on task t, the primary is the new task and every past task j < t contributes one secondary: k = t − 1 walls, one per past task as in GEM.

Rehearsal

Walls from stored examples

Wall j is the gradient of the loss on the stored examples of task j. The default primary is computed on the new minibatch joined by a replay minibatch from the whole memory: the loss ER trains on, up to how the memory is filled. The alternative is the new-only primary, which is GEM's.

The two leave the margin different amounts of work. A primary that contains replay already leans toward every wall, so fewer walls are active. When no wall is active, a PCD step with this primary is an ER step.

Regularization

Walls from EWC penalties: PCD-EWC

No example is stored. EWC merges all past tasks into one penalty with one weight. PCD-EWC keeps one penalty per past task, and the gradient of each is a wall:

\[ \begin{gathered} \Omega_j(\theta)=\tfrac{c}{2}\sum_{m} F_{j,m}\,(\theta_m-\theta^*_{j,m})^2, \\ g_j=\nabla\Omega_j(\theta)=c\,F_j\odot(\theta-\theta^*_j). \end{gathered} \]

The penalty scale c stands for something not known in advance. For EWC the best weight moves with c; in PCD-EWC the normalization should absorb it.

Priorities

A hierarchy among past tasks

Every rule sets τj = τ̄ wj with weights of mean one, so all rules spend the same mean margin and differ only in where they spend it. constant is the shared margin, oldest gives older tasks larger margins, and forgetting follows how far each task's stored-example loss has slipped. The sqrt schedule shrinks the shared margin to τ0/√k as tasks accumulate.

Relation to GEM

PCD and the GEM family

With τ = 0, raw gradients, the new-only primary and no rescaling, the program is GEM's problem. ε-SOFT-GEM is already a unit-normalized positive margin with one secondary. The PCD paper notes both. The question is which parts of PCD change continual-learning behaviour, in which setting, and at what cost.

Study design

Hypotheses first. Runs second. One reading rule.

This is a controlled comparison under one protocol, not a method proposal. The hypotheses were written before the runs that test them, every hyperparameter was fixed on a validation split, and the final runs, frozen in advance, were scored once on the test split.

RQ1

The margin and the primary

With a replay memory, does the margin help against GEM and against ER, and does it matter whether the primary also contains replayed examples?

H1 Compared with GEM at the same memory and number of updates, PCD with a validation-tuned margin has lower forgetting, and lowers learning accuracy by at most 2 points.

RQ2

Replacing EWC's weight

With no memory and walls built from EWC penalties, can PCD replace EWC's hand-tuned weight when the scale of the penalty is unknown?

H2a With the penalty scale treated as unknown and the same grid size for each method, PCD-EWC's validation-selected final accuracy, averaged over the scales, exceeds EWC's. H2b It is at least as good at each scale.

RQ3

A priority hierarchy

Should old tasks get different margins, set by their age or by how much each is being forgotten, and should the margin shrink as tasks accumulate?

H4a At the same mean margin, the forgetting rule has lower forgetting than one shared margin, at a cost of at most 2 points of learning accuracy. H4b The shrinking schedule has higher learning accuracy than a constant margin, at a cost of at most 1 point of forgetting.

RQ4 · outside the main runs

Task boundaries

What should PCD's running averages do when a new task starts: carry over, or restart?

H3 Restarting them lowers the normalization error after a boundary, and forgetting. Not among the main arms; tested afterwards at fixed settings.

  1. 4 NovemberH1, H2 and H3 are written, before any continual-learning run.
  2. 7 NovemberH4 is added when RQ3 joins the study: before any run with per-task margins, after the first exploration results for RQ1.
  3. Until 9 NovemberAn exploration on a validation split fixes every hyperparameter.
  4. 14 NovemberThe protocol and a pre-registration are frozen: the final arms, the comparisons and the reading rule.
  5. ThenThe first test-split run. The final runs tune nothing: they re-run fixed arms on test data and on two class orders that no earlier run had used.

The validation outcome was known before the test runs, and the pre-registration records the outcome that was expected. A clean negative result is a valid outcome.

The reading rule

Every comparison is paired: the two arms run with the same seed and the same class order, which gives nine differences. A difference is supported only if both conditions hold. Otherwise it is reported as not separated, whatever its mean.

  1. Size. The mean of the nine differences is larger in size than one sample standard deviation of those differences.
  2. Sign. At least seven of the nine differences have the sign of the mean.
  • supported passes both conditions
  • not separated fails at least one
  • Numbers on this page are mean ± standard deviation over the nine runs, or over the nine paired differences.
Benchmarks
Split MNIST: 5 tasks of 2 digits. Split CIFAR-100: 10 tasks of 10 classes. Class-incremental accuracy is the headline; task-incremental accuracy comes from the same runs.
Models and budget
A multilayer perceptron with two hidden layers of 256 units, 1 epoch per task; a reduced ResNet-18, 5 epochs per task. Batch size 32, plain SGD with no momentum and no weight decay.
Runs
44 arms × 9 runs = 396 final test-split runs, all finished. Each arm: seeds 0, 1, 2 crossed with three fixed class orders.
Memory
500 stored examples on Split MNIST; 500 and 2000 on Split CIFAR-100 (5 and 20 images per class). GEM and PCD keep the same class-balanced memory; ER keeps a reservoir sample of the same size.
Tuning
Ten percent of the training data held out as a validation split. Learning rate, GEM's memory strength, PCD's margin and EWC's weight were all chosen there. Several picks sit at a grid edge, so the paper writes "tuned on the same grid", never "fully tuned".
Further analysis
Experiments designed after the main comparisons, to explain them. They use fixed settings on the test split unless a setting is marked as tuned on validation, are read by the same rule, and change no verdict.

Results

Mostly negative. Fully reported.

RQ1 · Split MNIST

The margin and replay do the same job

With the new-only primary and τ = 0, PCD keeps only 56.3 ± 2.7 of final accuracy: the walls alone, without a margin, do not hold the old tasks. Either remedy repairs it. A margin of 0.75 adds +32.4 ± 2.4 points; putting replay in the primary adds +32.2 ± 2.9.

Once one is present the other adds little. With replay in the primary the margin is worth +0.9 ± 0.5 points (8/9 pairs), and at τ = 0.75 replay in the primary is worth +0.7 ± 0.8. They are substitutes.

H1 on Split MNIST: not supported. Against GEM, forgetting changes by −0.9 ± 1.5 at learning rate 0.1 (8/9; not separated) and −2.1 ± 0.9 at 0.03 (9/9; supported). H1 needs both pairs.

RQ1 · Split CIFAR-100

Level with replay. A margin costs accuracy.

With 500 stored examples, PCD minus ER is +0.4 ± 1.5 in final accuracy (6/9), with −5.6 ± 2.1 in forgetting and −5.3 ± 2.6 in learning accuracy: PCD forgets less and learns each new task less well, by about the same amount. It sits at another point of the same trade-off, not above it. Part of this trade may be an effect of the last batch of each epoch (Analysis 11). The chart can be switched between the three metrics.

PCD is ahead of GEM by +6.2 ± 2.1 (9/9), but ER beats GEM by a similar +6.3 ± 2.2, so the advantage is consistent with coming from replay in the update, not from the constraint. About half of both leads is an effect of the last batch of each epoch (Analysis 11).

Why so little changes: with replay in the primary and τ = 0, only 5.8% of the walls are active on CIFAR-100 (49.7% on Split MNIST), and a PCD step with no active wall is an ER step. The larger memory was run to test whether a very small memory explains the cost of the margin. It does not: the cost grows.

RQ2 · no memory

The one consistent gain: PCD-EWC over EWC

In class-incremental accuracy neither method leaves the floor (−0.6 ± 0.8 averaged over the scales), so RQ2 is read in task-incremental accuracy, as stated before the runs. There PCD-EWC ends above EWC at every penalty scale tried, and by +1.7 ± 0.9 points on average (9/9), with −1.8 ± 1.2 in forgetting.

Two cautions. Task-incremental Split MNIST is easy: fine-tuning alone reaches 89.1 ± 3.2. And the reason is not the hypothesized one. Scale invariance needs the running gradient size to be far above ε, and for small penalties it is not. The ε ratio, near zero when the normalization is scale invariant and near one when the constant dominates, is 100.0% at c = 10⁻² and 0.3% at c = 10².

H2a, H2b: supported in task-incremental accuracy only. What the data support is the practical statement: with the same small grid for each method, PCD-EWC ends at least as high as EWC at every scale tried.

RQ3 · priorities

The rules sit on top of the shared margin

At the same mean margin, the forgetting rule changes forgetting by a few tenths of a point in either direction, and no comparison with a shared margin is separated. On CIFAR-100 even the best rule does not beat no margin at all: the forgetting rule at mean margin 0.75 ends −1.6 ± 0.6 points of final accuracy from τ = 0.

The shrinking schedule on Split MNIST raises learning accuracy by +0.1 ± 0.0 at +0.6 ± 0.5 of forgetting and lowers final accuracy by −0.4 ± 0.4.

Reallocating a margin can only matter where the margin matters. With replay in the primary the whole margin is worth about a point on Split MNIST and is a loss on CIFAR-100, so there is little for a rule to redistribute.

Further analysis

Where the margin helps, and why it hurts.

Read this section differently. These experiments were designed after the main comparisons, to explain them, and they change no verdict. They are read by the same rule, many comparisons are read with no correction for multiplicity, and several supported effects are a few tenths of a point, so such small effects are tentative.

Analysis 01

With little memory, a margin matters. PCD's and GEM's do the same job.

On Split MNIST with 100 stored examples and every method retuned at that size, ER ends at 74.1 ± 1.1, GEM at 82.3 ± 0.9 and PCD at 82.7 ± 0.9. PCD minus ER is +8.6 ± 1.6 (9/9). PCD minus GEM is +0.4 ± 0.3 (8/9): it passes the rule, but it is under half a point and GEM's pick is at the edge of its grid, so the two are read as level.

What both need is a margin: PCD's is worth +4.6 ± 1.1 at the tuned setting, and GEM's pick moved to a memory strength of 4.

Analysis 02

Scale invariance holds once ε is small

At one fixed setting, PCD-EWC's task-incremental accuracy spans 2.46 points across the five penalty scales with the default constant and 0.13 points with ε = 10⁻¹⁶. EWC at one learning rate spans 3.5 points across four values of its strength, and at the largest only 4 of nine runs finish.

The mechanism behind H2 works at this setting; the default constant hid it at small penalties. This does not change the verdict on H2.

Analysis 03

The lead over EWC survives a wider grid

EWC's frozen pick was the largest strength that stayed stable in its grid. Retuned on a wider grid at c = 1, EWC's pick moves, and PCD-EWC stays +1.20 ± 0.69 ahead (9/9).

Only the baseline's grid was widened, so this check could only shrink the lead.

Analysis 04 · RQ4

Restarting the normalizer buys no final accuracy

The boundary modes reset and bank change nothing with 500 stored examples. With 100, reset lowers forgetting (−1.8 ± 0.5), though not through a better normalizer, and final accuracy is unchanged (+0.1 ± 0.3).

Analysis 05

The cost of a margin on CIFAR-100 is not the normalizer

Switching the scale normalizer off does not remove the cost: the un-normalized margin ends −1.3 ± 0.9 from τ = 0, still supported. With 200 stored examples the margin does not become a gain either (−0.4 ± 0.6, nothing retuned).

This rests on one value of τ whose meaning changes with the normalizer, so it does not show that the normalizer plays no part.

Analysis 06

Three dials on one trade-off

GEM without its memory strength ends −33.2 ± 3.1 points from GEM with it on Split MNIST: PCD at τ = 0 without replay behaves as GEM without its memory strength. Against a GEM with memory strength 2, above its grid, PCD's lead in final accuracy at learning rate 0.03 is not separated (+0.3 ± 0.7).

PCD's margin, replay inside the primary and GEM's memory strength all push the update toward the old tasks. The margin is a smooth way to move GEM's wall, not an independent source of protection.

Analysis 07

On CIFAR-100 the walls add nothing over replay on the same memory

With its walls switched off, PCD takes ER's update on PCD's memory. On CIFAR-100 the walls then change final accuracy by −0.0 ± 1.1, and the memory accounts for the whole trade against ER: −5.7 ± 2.4 in forgetting and −5.5 ± 2.7 in learning accuracy.

On Split MNIST with 100 stored examples the walls with their margin are worth +10.6 ± 1.8.

Analysis 08

FedPCD's normalization: more on Split MNIST, not selected on CIFAR-100

Normalizing each gradient by its own length at every step, as FedPCD does, makes the margin 0.75 worth +2.1 ± 0.9 on Split MNIST, against +0.9 ± 0.5 with the running average.

On CIFAR-100 a margin of 0.1 gains +1.1 ± 0.9 on the test split, with task-incremental accuracy −4.1 ± 2.0, but the validation split does not select it over τ = 0.

Analysis 09

Why a margin costs accuracy on CIFAR-100: three explanations, none confirmed

Switching the normalizer off does not remove the cost. The margin does not lower the mean loss on the stored examples, although it lowers it in 8 of 9 pairs. A wall sees its task under that task's own BatchNorm statistics, but walls computed under running statistics make the cost larger (−4.7 ± 2.2 against −1.8 ± 1.1), not smaller.

With those walls the margin trades learning accuracy (−31.0 ± 2.1) for forgetting (−27.0 ± 2.6), and the margin picked on validation is not separated from τ = 0.

Analysis 10

Under Adam, the CIFAR-100 answers return

The PCD paper trains with Adam, so the central comparisons were repeated under it. On Split MNIST the margin 0.75 is worth +1.6 ± 0.6 (9/9), and PCD ends +4.1 ± 0.9 above ER. A tuned GEM obtains +3.4 ± 0.8 of that lead.

On CIFAR-100, Adam with a loss over all outputs kept every arm from learning the last two tasks, so that comparison is not valid. With the loss restricted to the classes seen so far, every task is learned and ER ends at 18.0%. PCD with its selected margin then ends −0.2 ± 1.2 from ER (not separated), the walls change final accuracy by −0.1 ± 0.8, and a margin of 0.75 changes it by −0.8 ± 0.5 (supported). A tuned GEM ends −4.8 ± 0.8 from ER, and PCD ends +4.6 ± 1.1 above GEM, about as much as ER does. This comparison changes the loss as well as the optimizer.

The other three questions were repeated on Split MNIST. The forgetting rule raises final accuracy by +0.2 ± 0.1 and +0.2 ± 0.2 in two of four comparisons. With 500 stored examples, reset changes forgetting by −0.9 ± 0.3 but not final accuracy, and on CIFAR-100 it ends +0.1 ± 1.0 from carry. PCD-EWC leads online EWC in task-IL by a separated amount only at c = 0.1, by +6.9 ± 4.9. Its average lead over the five scales, +1.4 ± 1.5, is not separated.

Analysis 11

The last batch of each epoch handicapped GEM

Every CIFAR-100 epoch ends on a batch of 8 images, which lowers the learning accuracy of arms that take it without replay. With that batch dropped in every arm, PCD leads GEM by +3.3 ± 1.3 (9/9), about half of the lead in the main runs, and now through lower forgetting (−4.3 ± 1.9).

ER leads GEM by a similar +3.8 ± 1.3, and PCD stays level with ER (−0.5 ± 0.8). The ranking of the methods does not change.

Analysis 12

About half of the lead over EWC is per-task storage

An EWC with one penalty per task, tuned at c = 1, ends +0.65 ± 0.41 above online EWC in task-incremental accuracy. PCD-EWC ends +0.55 ± 0.37 above that per-task EWC (9/9).

So about half of PCD-EWC's lead belongs to keeping one penalty per task, and about half remains against the matched baseline, at one penalty scale.

Analysis 13

Without batch statistics, the cost is no longer separated

With every BatchNorm layer replaced by group normalization and the learning rate retuned, the margin 0.75 changes final accuracy by −0.5 ± 0.7, which is not separated. It still raises forgetting by +4.1 ± 2.1.

That network is much weaker (12.7% without the margin, 12.2% with it), so this is consistent with a role of batch statistics but does not establish it.

Analysis 14 · RQ3

The priority rules do not help where the margin helps

On CIFAR-100 with the new-only primary the margin itself is worth +4.4 ± 2.0. Even there, neither rule improves on the shared margin: −0.3 ± 1.6 for oldest and +0.3 ± 1.9 for forgetting.

The oldest rule also does not move accuracy toward the oldest tasks.

Analysis 15

The walls are expensive per step

PCD and GEM compute one extra gradient per past task at every step, on all stored examples of that task. Alone on the GPU, ER takes about 14 ms per step on CIFAR-100. The fastest PCD run took 95 ms with 500 stored examples and 333 ms with 2000.

The PCD runs shared the GPU with other runs, so these figures overstate its cost somewhat.

Scope

What this study shows, and what it does not claim.

  • PCD with τ = 0 is GEM's constraint. With raw gradients and the new-only primary it reduces to GEM and A-GEM, and a normalized positive margin is ε-SOFT-GEM. The study asks which parts of PCD change continual-learning behaviour.
  • The constraint is a first-order statement. A positive dot product means the secondary loss decreases only for a small enough step on a smooth loss. The secondary losses are computed on a few stored examples or on a penalty, and progress on these stand-ins is not a guarantee on the true loss of a past task. PCD's result on where its direction vanishes concerns fixed points of the update, not the convergence of a training run. None of this rules out forgetting.
  • Two small benchmarks, one network each, at most ten tasks. The main runs use plain SGD with one or five epochs per task, while the PCD paper trains with Adam for 300 epochs. The runs under Adam keep the short schedule, and they compare the priority rules on Split MNIST only.
  • Small grids. Several picks sit at a grid edge or are near-ties. Class order 0 was also the tuning order, so every comparison is repeated on the other two orders alone: of 105 readings, 12 change.
  • Not the same memory contents as ER. ER keeps a reservoir sample that can include the current task; PCD and GEM keep a class-balanced memory of past tasks. With 2000 stored examples on CIFAR-100, PCD and GEM also differ in BatchNorm handling.
  • The regularization result is narrow. RQ2 holds in task-incremental accuracy only, on the easiest benchmark. The EWC baseline is the framework's online EWC, which merges all past tasks into one penalty while PCD-EWC keeps one wall per task. Against an EWC with one penalty per task, about half of the lead remains, at one penalty scale. Under Adam, the lead over online EWC is separated at only one of five scales.
  • All other conclusions concern class-incremental learning with a small memory. The tuned low-memory comparison was run on Split MNIST only, and GEM's pick there is at the largest memory strength tried.
  • RQ4 was not among the main arms, and every further analysis is exploratory and cannot change a verdict.
  • The constraint is expensive. PCD and GEM compute one gradient per past task on top of the primary's at every step, against one for ER, and are many times slower per step. Under plain SGD on CIFAR-100 the projection buys no final accuracy over replay alone.
  • The cause of the margin's cost on CIFAR-100 is not identified. Three explanations were tested (the normalizer, over-fitting of the stored examples, BatchNorm statistics in the wall passes) and none is confirmed. In a network without batch statistics the cost in final accuracy is no longer separated, but that network is much weaker and the margin still raises forgetting.

Natural next tests for PCD: a network without batch statistics that matches the accuracy of the BatchNorm network, secondaries that are not a handful of stored images (a held-out memory, distillation targets), longer task sequences, and a full study under Adam with the long training schedule of the PCD paper.
